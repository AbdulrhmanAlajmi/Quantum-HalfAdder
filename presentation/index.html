Building block nº1

The qubit — a bit that learned to dream

Classical bit

0 1

0 or 1

A switch. On or off. Every photo, song and app you own is a mountain of these definite answers.

Qubit

|0⟩
|1⟩

|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩

A wave of probability. Until measured, it lives on a sphere of possibilities between 0 and 1 — direction, phase and all.

Each added qubit doubles the state space: 300 qubits hold 2³⁰⁰ amplitudes — more than there are atoms in the observable universe.

Quantum property nº1

Superposition — everything, everywhere, until you look

A spinning coin isn't heads or tails — it's a blur of both. A qubit in superposition explores many answers at once; measurement forces it to choose exactly one. The art of a quantum algorithm is choreographing the blur so that wrong answers cancel out and right ones reinforce.

Quantum property nº2

Entanglement — “spooky action at a distance”

one shared state
↑
↓
any distance — a lab bench or a galaxy apart

Entangled qubits stop being individuals: measure one and the other's outcome is instantly correlated, however far apart they are. Einstein called it spooky; experiments by Aspect, Clauser and Zeilinger proved it real — and won the 2022 Nobel Prize. It is the resource behind quantum networks, error correction and teleportation.

Quantum property nº3

Quantum teleportation — beam the state, not the atom

STEP 01

Share entanglement

Alice and Bob each take one qubit of an entangled pair — an invisible thread stretched between them.

STEP 02

Measure & send 2 bits

Alice measures her mystery qubit together with her half of the pair, destroying it — and phones Bob two ordinary classical bits.

STEP 03

Rebuild the state

Bob applies a tiny correction based on those bits — and his qubit becomes the original state, exactly.

No matter moves and nothing beats light speed — the classical phone call is mandatory. In 2017 China's Micius satellite teleported photon states over 1,200 km from Tibet to orbit. This is the backbone of the future quantum internet.

Application 01 / 05 · Cybersecurity

The code-breaker that forces better locks

  • Shor's algorithm lets a large quantum computer factor huge numbers — the exact task RSA encryption bets is impossible. Most of today's internet security has a quantum expiry date.
  • “Harvest now, decrypt later” is happening today: adversaries stockpile encrypted traffic to unlock it once quantum hardware matures.
  • The fix is shipping: in 2024 NIST standardized post-quantum encryption (ML-KEM, ML-DSA) — already rolling out in Chrome, iMessage and Signal — while banks pilot quantum key distribution, where physics itself exposes any eavesdropper.

Application 02 / 05 · Space

Quantum physics goes into orbit

  • Micius (China, 2016) — the first quantum satellite — distributed entangled photons across 1,200 km and secured an intercontinental video call between Beijing and Vienna with quantum keys.
  • Navigation without GPS: quantum accelerometers and atomic clocks track position from physics alone — un-jammable backup navigation for ships, aircraft and submarines, already flight-tested by the UK and Boeing.
  • NASA's Cold Atom Lab on the ISS runs the coldest experiments in the known universe — Bose-Einstein condensates in microgravity — a testbed for quantum sensors that could map gravity, water tables and dark matter.

Application 03 / 05 · Telecom

Building the quantum internet

  • The Beijing–Shanghai backbone — over 2,000 km of fiber with 32 trusted nodes — has carried quantum-encrypted traffic for banks and government since 2017; merged with Micius, it forms a 4,600 km space-ground network.
  • Carriers are in: SK Telecom secures its 5G subscriber authentication with quantum randomness; BT & Toshiba run a commercial QKD metro network in London; Telefónica, SKT and Deutsche Telekom pilot quantum-safe links.
  • Next: quantum repeaters — devices that entangle across segments to span continents without trusted middle-men. Delft's QuTech already linked three cities in the first true multi-node quantum network (2022).

Application 04 / 05 · Logistics

Untangling the world's hardest puzzles

  • Routing explodes fast: one truck visiting just 15 cities has over a trillion possible routes. Optimization is where quantum annealers and hybrid algorithms shine first.
  • Real pilots: Volkswagen quantum-routed Lisbon's buses live during WebSummit 2019 (D-Wave); DHL prototypes quantum cargo-loading; the Port of Los Angeles trials quantum-inspired scheduling to cut crane idle time.
  • Airbus & Boeing explore quantum optimization for wing design, fleet allocation and supply chains — where shaving 1% off global freight costs is worth billions per year.

Application 05 / 05 · Health

Simulating nature to design medicine

  • Molecules are quantum objects — simulating them exactly is exponentially hard for classical machines. Feynman's original 1981 argument for quantum computers was precisely this.
  • Cleveland Clinic × IBM installed the first quantum computer dedicated to healthcare research (2023), targeting protein structure and drug screening; Roche, Boehringer Ingelheim and Moderna run quantum drug-discovery programs.
  • Beyond drugs: quantum sensors detect the brain's faint magnetic fields at room temperature (wearable MEG scanners), and quantum machine learning is being tested on MRI reconstruction and early-cancer imaging.
